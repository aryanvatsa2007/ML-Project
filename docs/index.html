The question

For one stock on day t, using only information available at the close of day t, can we predict how much its price will move over the next 21 trading days?

Target
The log of the realised volatility: the standard deviation of daily log-returns over days t+1 to t+21. We use the log because volatility is positive and right-skewed.
Unit of observation
One row = one stock on one day. Stocks are pooled into a single table.
Type of problem
Supervised regression on time-ordered data.
Limits of the claim
We forecast how large price moves will be, not their direction. This is not a trading signal or financial advice.

Data

Quality checkResult

Access: public Yahoo Finance data, downloaded by src/data.py. No account, API key or private data is used.

Method

Features (all use only the past)

Past volatility over 5, 21 and 63 days; returns over 5 and 21 days; distance below the 63-day high; recent volume relative to normal; yesterday's absolute return; and the market's (SPY) volatility and return. Volatility and volume features are logged.

Avoiding leakage

  • The data are split in time: train up to , validation (tuning) during 2022, test afterwards, used once.
  • A 21-day gap is dropped at the end of train and validation, because the target looks 21 days ahead and windows would otherwise overlap.
  • The standard scaler sits inside a pipeline, so it learns its mean and spread from training rows only.
  • Automated tests in tests/ check that features ignore the future.

Baselines and models

ModelIdea
Historical meanAlways predict the average volatility seen in training. The weakest sensible baseline.
PersistenceNext month's volatility equals the last 21 days'. Hard to beat, because volatility clusters.
HAR (OLS)Linear regression on short, medium and long past volatility only. A standard finance benchmark.
RidgeLinear regression on all features with an L2 penalty to control overfitting.
Random ForestMany decision trees, averaged. Can capture non-linear effects and interactions.

Hyper-parameters (Ridge alpha, forest depth and leaf size) are chosen on the validation year. The chosen models are then refitted on train and validation together and evaluated on the test period one time.

Limitations

  • Ten large US stocks, mostly one market regime. Results may not carry over to small stocks, other countries or crises not seen in training.
  • Models are trained on past data and assume the relationship between features and future volatility stays similar.
  • Turning log-forecasts back into volatility with exp() slightly under-estimates the average (Jensen's inequality).
  • Correlations are taken from the recent past and are not forecast; they tend to rise in crashes, so portfolio risk can be underestimated when it matters most.
  • Value at risk assumes normally distributed returns, which understates tail losses.
  • Overlapping 21-day targets mean neighbouring rows are not independent, so small differences between models should not be over-read.
